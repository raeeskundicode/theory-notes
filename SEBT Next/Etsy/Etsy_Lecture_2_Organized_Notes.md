Etsy is an online marketplace where sellers can sell physical as well as **digital products**.

The main focus of the lecture/course is:

> **Selling Digital Products on Etsy**

Digital products can include:

- Templates
- Planners
- Worksheets
- Printable designs
- Invitations
- Digital artwork
- Social media templates
- Other downloadable files

The instructor also mentioned other websites and dropshipping platforms, but the main focus is Etsy digital products.

# 2. Why Do Most Etsy Sellers Fail?

The lecture identifies several reasons why Etsy sellers don't receive orders.

The most important reason is generally:

> **A poorly optimized listing.**

A listing is the product page that a customer sees when they find your product.

A good listing needs several elements:

- Title
- Product images
- Description
- Tags
- Keywords
- Pricing
- SEO
- Visual presentation
- Branding

If these elements are not properly optimized, your product may not get enough visibility or clicks.

---

# 3. Poor Listing = Fewer Orders

### What makes a listing bad?

A listing can perform poorly because of:

- Incorrect keywords
- Weak title
- Poor product images
- Poor image optimization
- Weak description
- Wrong tags
- Incorrect pricing
- Poor SEO
- Lack of branding

For example, imagine you are selling a **wedding invitation template**.

If your title is simply:

> "Wedding Invitation"

you may be missing many useful buyer keywords.

A more targeted title might describe the actual product and buyer intent, such as:

> "Minimalist Wedding Invitation Template | Editable Wedding Invite"

The important lesson is:

> **Don't just create a product. Create and optimize the listing around what the buyer is searching for.**

---

# 4. SEO Is Extremely Important

**SEO = Search Engine Optimization.**

On Etsy, SEO helps your product appear when buyers search for something.

The instructor emphasized that many sellers have a product but don't understand how to optimize it.

### Important SEO Areas

You should pay attention to:

- Title
- Tags
- Keywords
- Description
- Product category
- Images
- Buyer search intent

The goal is to use **keywords that your potential buyers actually search for.**

---

# 5. Exact Buyer Keywords

One important concept from the lecture is:

> **Use exact buyer keywords.**

Don't only think about what *you* call your product.

Think about:

> **"What words will my customer type into Etsy's search box?"**

### Example

Suppose you create a printable planner.

You might call it:

> "My Productivity Planner"

But a buyer might search:

> "Printable Daily Planner"

The second phrase may be more aligned with buyer search intent.

Therefore:

**Seller's terminology ≠ necessarily buyer's terminology.**

---

# 6. Product Images and Image Optimization

Images play a **major role** in Etsy selling.

The customer usually sees your images before reading your entire description.

Therefore, your images should:

- Clearly show the product
- Look professional
- Be easy to understand
- Communicate the product's benefits
- Follow a consistent visual style
- Work well on mobile screens

### Common Mistake

Some sellers put **too much text on their listing images**.

Even if the product has good SEO, excessive text can make the listing visually unattractive and difficult to understand.

So:

> **Good SEO + poor visuals = potentially poor performance.**

---

# 7. Visual Branding

The instructor emphasized that many students/sellers miss **visual branding**.

Your shop should have a consistent visual identity.

This includes:

- Colors
- Fonts
- Image style
- Layout
- Product presentation
- Overall shop appearance

### Why Branding Matters

Imagine visiting two Etsy shops.

**Shop A:**  
Every product has different colors, fonts, and styles.

**Shop B:**  
All products use a consistent color palette, typography, and design style.

Shop B generally looks more professional and recognizable.

Therefore:

> **Your shop should look like a brand, not a collection of random products.**

---

# 8. CTR — Click-Through Rate

**CTR (Click-Through Rate)** tells you how often people **click your listing after seeing it**.

For example:

If 1,000 people see your listing and 50 click it:

**CTR = 5%**

Formula:

> **CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100**

A good thumbnail/image and relevant title can encourage more people to click.

---

# 9. Conversion Rate

Conversion tells you how many visitors actually **buy your product**.

For example:

100 people visit your listing.

5 people purchase.

Your conversion rate is:

**5%**

Formula:

> **Conversion Rate = Purchases ÷ Visits × 100**

### Why CTR and Conversion Matter

The lecture explains that customer behavior such as clicks and purchases can influence how your listing performs in Etsy search.

The basic funnel is:

**Impression → Click → Visit → Purchase → Review**

Therefore:

> **Getting impressions isn't enough. You need clicks and sales.**

# 12. Etsy Fees

The lecture mentions an **Etsy one-time fee of $19** and a **6.5% charge per sale**.

These figures can change over time and may depend on the type of fee or transaction, so they should be checked against Etsy's current fee schedule before making financial decisions.

The important lesson is:

> **Always calculate Etsy fees before deciding your product price and profit margin.**

# 13. Mobile Optimization

The lecture emphasizes that a large proportion of Etsy buyers use mobile devices.

Therefore:

> **Your listing must look good on mobile.**

When designing listing images, check:

- Is the text readable?
- Is the product clearly visible?
- Is the main information immediately understandable?
- Does the thumbnail look attractive?
- Is anything being cut off?

### Important Habit

Don't only check your listing from your desktop.

> **Check your listing as if you were a buyer using a mobile phone.**

# 14. Competitor Research

Your competitors are generally sellers offering products in the **same niche**.

Before creating your product, study successful competitors.

Look at:

- Product designs
- Number of listings
- Prices
- Titles
- Descriptions
- Images
- Branding
- Product bundles
- Reviews
- Sales indicators where available

### But Don't Copy Them

The purpose of competitor research is to understand:

> **What is already working in the market?**

Then create something that is **different, useful, and better positioned**.

# 19. Writing the Product Description

The lecture recommends keeping description paragraphs short.

A useful rule from your notes is:

> **Keep each paragraph within approximately 5 lines.**

Why?

Because large blocks of text are difficult to read, especially on mobile.

A better structure is:

**Short paragraph**

**Product features**

- Feature 1
- Feature 2
- Feature 3

**What's included**

- File 1
- File 2
- File 3

**How to use**

Step 1  
Step 2  
Step 3

This makes the listing easier to scan.

---

# 20. Check Your Listing as a Buyer

One of the most useful practical points in the lecture is:

> **After completing your listing, look at it as a buyer.**

Don't look at it only as the seller.

Ask yourself:

### Title

"Does this immediately tell me what the product is?"

### Image

"Would I click this?"

### Description

"Do I understand what I'm buying?"

### Price

"Does the value seem reasonable?"

### Product

"Does it solve my problem?"

### Mobile

"Does everything look good on my phone?"

This simple exercise can reveal problems that you didn't notice while creating the listing.

# 21. Etsy Reverse Engineering

The lecture introduces **reverse engineering** as a research method.

Instead of randomly creating products, study existing successful shops/listings and work backward.

For example:

**Successful Product**  
↓  
What niche is it in?  
↓  
What keywords does it target?  
↓  
What is its design style?  
↓  
How many listings does the shop have?  
↓  
What is the pricing?  
↓  
How are the images designed?  
↓  
How is the title written?  
↓  
How is the description structured?  
↓  
What can I learn from this?

This is **market research**, not copying.

# 22. Finding Younger Shops With Fewer Listings

Your notes mention looking for shops that are:

- Less than approximately one year old
- Have relatively few listings
- Yet appear to be getting sales/attention

This can be useful because such shops may reveal **recently working strategies**, rather than strategies that worked many years ago.

However, don't assume that a young shop with sales automatically means its strategy will work for you.

Use it as **research evidence**.

# 25. Important Mistakes to Avoid

Based on the lecture, pay special attention to these mistakes:

### ❌ Poor SEO

Using keywords that buyers don't search for.

### ❌ Poor Images

Having an excellent product but unattractive presentation.

### ❌ Too Much Text in Images

Making thumbnails crowded and difficult to understand.

### ❌ No Visual Branding

Using random colors, fonts, and designs.

### ❌ Ignoring Mobile

Creating a listing that looks good only on desktop.

### ❌ Copying Competitors

Research competitors, but don't simply duplicate their products.

### ❌ Depending Completely on Tools

Use tools alongside manual research.

### ❌ Not Checking Your Listing as a Buyer

Always view the finished listing from the customer's perspective.

### ❌ Ignoring Bundles

Related products can sometimes be combined into a more valuable offer.
